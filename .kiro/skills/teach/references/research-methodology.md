# Research Before Teaching (mandatory)

Before writing any lesson, complete a research phase. A lesson without sourced research is teaching from memory — which means teaching things that might be wrong, outdated, or missing blind spots.

## Process

1. **Identify subtopics** (3-6) an expert would consider before explaining this concept
2. **Dispatch research** per subtopic (2+ sources, practitioner perspectives, warnings)
3. **Populate RESOURCES.md** with verified sources and trust levels
4. **Write the lesson** — every claim traces to a finding

## What to research for any topic

| Category | Questions |
|----------|-----------|
| Domain fundamentals | Core concepts? Accepted terminology? |
| Current best practices | What does the community recommend TODAY? |
| Common mistakes | What do practitioners warn newcomers about? |
| Ecosystem/tools | Current options? Maintained? Deprecated? |
| Controversies/tradeoffs | Where do experts disagree? |
| Adjacent concerns | What will the learner hit next? |

## Adequacy criteria

- 2+ independent sources per major claim
- At least one practitioner source (not just official docs)
- Explicit search for warnings/anti-patterns
- Sources are current (check dates)
- RESOURCES.md populated BEFORE writing

## The "look around corners" test

"If an expert read my lesson, would they say it's right and not missing anything important?"

## What does NOT count as research

- Parametric memory
- A single blog post with no corroboration
- Official docs alone without practitioner perspective
- Research that only confirms what you already "know"
