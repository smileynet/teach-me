Programming Basics — Structuring Your Training Week

Lesson 3 · Workout Fundamentals · ~10 min read
Win: You can design a concrete 3-day training week that applies progressive overload with appropriate volume, and explain why it's structured that way.
After this lesson: You know how many sets, reps, and sessions per week are appropriate for a beginner. You understand why full-body training 3x/week is the default, what "enough volume" means, and why linear progression is all you need for 3-9 months.

The Problem: Overload Without a Plan is Chaos

You know you need to increase demands over time (Lesson 1) and rest between sessions (Lesson 2). But how much work per session? How many exercises? How often? Without answering these, you'll either do too little (no overload signal) or too much (under-recovery). Programming turns principles into a schedule. [ACSM Position Stand 2009]

Why Full-Body 3×/Week (Not a Split)

The internet is full of 5-6 day "bro splits" (chest day, back day, leg day...). Research says: for beginners, full-body and splits produce identical gains when volume is equal. A 2024 meta-analysis found no difference in hypertrophy or strength between the two approaches. [JSCR 2024]

Full-body wins practically because:

  • More practice. You squat 3×/week instead of 1× — motor learning accelerates.
  • Miss-proof. Skip one session and you've still hit everything twice that week.
  • Simpler. Same structure every session with small variations. Less to think about.
  • Matches recovery. Beginners don't generate enough muscle damage per session to need 7 days of rest for one muscle group.

Splits become useful later — when your volume per muscle is too high to fit into 3 sessions. That's an intermediate problem.

The Beginner Template

A complete full-body session for a beginner covers 5 movement patterns:

PatternExampleSets × Reps
Squat (knee-dominant)Goblet squat or barbell squat3 × 8-12
Hinge (hip-dominant)Romanian deadlift or trap bar deadlift3 × 8-12
Horizontal pushBench press or dumbbell press3 × 8-12
Horizontal pullBarbell row or cable row3 × 8-12
Vertical push or pullOverhead press or lat pulldown3 × 8-12

That's 15 sets per session, 45 minutes. Three sessions per week = 9-15 sets per muscle group weekly (compounds hit multiple muscles). Enough for growth, recoverable between sessions, simple to progress.

Linear Progression: The Only System You Need (For Now)

The rule: If you completed all prescribed sets and reps with good form → add 2.5kg next session. If you couldn't complete the minimum reps → repeat the same weight.

Why this works for beginners: your nervous system and muscles adapt fast enough to support session-to-session increases. Neural adaptations dominate the first 4-8 weeks — you get stronger without muscles getting bigger because your brain gets better at recruiting existing muscle fibers.

Linear progression works for 3-9 months depending on the individual. When it stops working (can't add weight despite sleeping and eating well), that's the signal to graduate to intermediate programming — weekly rather than session-based progression. But don't solve that problem before you have it. [r/Fitness Wiki]

What Doesn't Matter (Yet)

Check Your Understanding

Exercise: A friend shows you their new program: 6 days per week, each session is 2 hours with 25+ sets, and they've been training for 3 weeks. What's likely to happen, and what would you suggest instead?

Hint

Think about the relationship between volume, recovery capacity, and training age. What does a beginner's MRV look like compared to what this program demands?

Key points to hit

Should mention: (1) they're probably exceeding their MRV (maximum recoverable volume) — beginners need far less volume than they think, (2) high frequency with high volume per session means insufficient recovery between sessions, (3) 2-hour sessions suggest either too much volume or too many exercises. Suggestion: 3 full-body sessions, 5 compound movements each, 3 sets of 8-12 — less than half their current volume but likely to produce better results because they can actually recover from it.
