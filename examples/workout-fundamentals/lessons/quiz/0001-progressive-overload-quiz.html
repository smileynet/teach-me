Quiz: Progressive Overload

explain

Why does doing the same workout every week eventually stop producing results?

Should mention: (1) the body adapts to match the demand (accommodation), (2) without increasing stimulus, there's no signal to continue adapting. It's biology working as designed — not a failure of effort.

apply

A friend says they've been going to the gym for 6 months but aren't getting stronger. What's the most likely explanation?

Should mention: (1) they're probably not increasing the demand — same weights, same reps every session, (2) without overload, the body has no reason to adapt further. The diagnostic question: "Are you lifting more than 3 months ago?" If no, that's the problem.

Someone recommends a specific exercise, exact rest times, and a supplement stack. How would you evaluate whether any of it matters?

Should mention: (1) ask "does this help me progressively overload over time?", (2) if it doesn't affect the overload trajectory, it's likely noise. The fitness industry sells complexity because simple doesn't generate content.

explain

Why can't a text-based learning system fully replace a coach for learning to squat?

Should mention: (1) squatting is procedural knowledge — motor patterns requiring physical practice and proprioceptive feedback, (2) complex movements benefit from real-time form correction. Text teaches the WHY; the body learns the HOW through repetition with feedback.

predict

Someone follows a "muscle confusion" program — different exercises every session, no tracking. What will happen after 3 months?

Should mention: (1) without tracking, there's no progressive overload signal — the body has no reason to adapt beyond handling random moderate stimuli, (2) they'll get general fitness but not meaningful strength gains, (3) each session is "novel" (soreness) without being progressive (heavier over time). Key: novelty ≠ overload.

quick-check

Name the 3 methods of progressive overload and when to use each:

(1) Add weight — when you hit the top of your rep range with good form. (2) Add reps — when you can't add weight yet (smallest plate constraint). (3) Add sets — when stalled on both weight and reps. Trend upward over weeks/months.
