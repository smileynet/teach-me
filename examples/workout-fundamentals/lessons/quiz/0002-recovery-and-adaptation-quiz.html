Why is sleep the single highest-leverage recovery factor?

Should mention: (1) ~70% of daily growth hormone secretion happens during deep sleep, (2) sleep deprivation reduces testosterone, impairs muscle protein synthesis, increases cortisol. No supplement compensates for bad sleep.

Your lifts have stalled for 2 weeks and you feel sluggish. A gym buddy says "push harder." What's actually happening?

Should mention: (1) likely overreaching — accumulated fatigue exceeding recovery, (2) "push harder" is the exact wrong response, (3) correct: deload week (reduce volume 40-60%, keep moderate intensity, 5-7 days). Doing LESS produces better results because fatigue dissipates.

How well could you explain this?

Section: When to Back Off · Tags: overreaching, deload, stalling

Explain the supercompensation cycle and why training timing matters.

Should mention: (1) four phases: baseline → training drops capacity → recovery → overshoot past baseline, (2) train in the supercompensation window = stack gains, (3) too soon = fatigue, too late = back to baseline. For beginners: ~48-72h per muscle group → 3×/week full-body.

How well could you explain this?

Section: Supercompensation · Tags: supercompensation, timing, adaptation
predict

A beginner trains the same muscles every day (no rest days) and eats plenty of protein. What will happen over 4 weeks?

Should mention: (1) initial progress from neural adaptation, then stalling, (2) training before recovery completes means they're always in the fatigue phase — never reaching supercompensation, (3) likely outcome: overreaching symptoms (persistent fatigue, joint ache, motivation drop) within 2-3 weeks. Protein doesn't fix insufficient rest time.

How well could you explain this?

Section: Supercompensation · Tags: overreaching, rest-days, recovery-timing
