Why does research show full-body 3×/week is preferable to a bro split for beginners?

Should mention: (1) 2024 meta-analysis shows identical gains when volume is equal, (2) full-body wins practically: more motor learning (3×/wk practice), miss-proof, simpler scheduling, matches beginner recovery. Splits are a scheduling tool, not a growth tool.

A friend's program: 6 days/week, 2-hour sessions, 25+ sets, 3 weeks in. What's likely to happen?

Should mention: (1) exceeding MRV — beginners need far less volume, (2) insufficient recovery at that frequency + volume, (3) suggest: 3 full-body sessions, 5 compounds, 3×8-12 — less than half the volume but better results because recovery is possible.

explain

Why does linear progression work for beginners but not intermediates?

Should mention: (1) beginners adapt fast enough for session-to-session increases, (2) neural adaptations dominate first 4-8 weeks — strength grows faster than muscle, (3) intermediates adapt slower, need weekly progression because recovery rates decline with training age.

explain

What are the 5 movement patterns and why organize by pattern rather than exercise?

Should mention: (1) squat, hinge, horizontal push, horizontal pull, vertical push/pull, (2) patterns ensure balanced development, (3) specific exercises matter less — bench ≈ machine press for growth. Pick what you'll do consistently and can overload.

predict

A beginner adds 5kg to every lift every session (instead of 2.5kg). What happens after 6 weeks?

Should mention: (1) they'll stall much sooner — jumping too fast outpaces adaptation rate, (2) form will break down as weight exceeds their capacity to handle it safely, (3) they'll think they've "finished" linear progression when really they just overshot. 2.5kg works because it matches the rate a beginner's body can adapt between sessions.

How well could you explain this?
