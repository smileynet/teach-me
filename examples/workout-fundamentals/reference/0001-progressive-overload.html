Progressive Overload

Reference · Companion to Lesson 1

Core Concept

Increase the demand over time or the body stops adapting. This is the single mechanism behind all training gains. Everything else is implementation detail.

How to Overload

MethodWhen to useExample
Add weightHit top of rep range with good form60kg × 8 → 62.5kg × 8
Add repsCan't add weight yet (smallest plate is 2.5kg)50kg × 6 → 50kg × 8
Add setsStalled on weight and reps3 sets → 4 sets

Beginner Protocol (linear progression)

  1. Pick a rep target (e.g., 3 sets × 8 reps)
  2. If you complete all sets at target → add 2.5kg next session
  3. If you can't complete 3×6 → stay at this weight until you can
  4. Repeat for 4-9 months (this is "linear progression")

What Doesn't Matter Much (Yet)

Commonly stressedWhat research says
Specific exercisesBench vs machine press → similar hypertrophy. Pick what you'll do consistently.
Exact rep range6-30 reps all produce growth near failure. 6-15 is time-efficient sweet spot.
Training to failure1-3 reps from failure = equivalent results with less fatigue (Vieira 2022).
Nutrient timingTotal daily protein matters more than the "anabolic window."
Complex periodizationLinear progression works until it doesn't (4-9 months). Don't complicate early.

Decision Aid

"Am I making progress?"

  • Lifting more weight/reps than 4 weeks ago → yes, keep going
  • Same weights for 3+ weeks → stalled — increase something or deload
  • Weights going down → recovery issue — check sleep, food, stress

"Should I try [new thing]?"

  • Does it help you progressively overload → maybe useful
  • Does it not affect overload → probably noise

Pursue Alongside

  • 2-3 coaching sessions for squat/deadlift/press form
  • Effort calibration — learn what "near failure" feels like by doing it
  • Accountability system — partner, schedule, or simple log (adherence > knowledge)
  • Form checks — film working sets, post to r/formcheck
