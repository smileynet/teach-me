# Resources

## Primary Sources

| Resource | Type | Trust | Notes |
|----------|------|-------|-------|
| [ACSM Position Stand: Resistance Training (2009, updated 2023)](https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/) | Position stand | Authoritative | The evidence summary for RT prescription. Progressive overload, volume, frequency recommendations. |
| [Schoenfeld et al. — Dose-Response of RT on Hypertrophy (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/) | Meta-analysis | Authoritative | Graded dose-response: more sets = more growth, but diminishing returns. 10+ sets/week for experienced. |
| [Schoenfeld et al. — Training Frequency (2016)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/) | Meta-analysis | Authoritative | 2x/week > 1x/week for hypertrophy. Frequency is a volume distribution tool. |
| [r/Fitness Wiki](https://thefitness.wiki/) | Community wiki | High | Excellent beginner guide. Programs, nutrition, FAQ. Community-vetted, regularly updated. |
| [Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/) | Blog/Research | High | Greg Nuckols. Evidence-based, primary-source citing, free training guides. |
| [MASS Research Review](https://www.massmembers.com/) | Monthly review | High | Schoenfeld, Helms, Trexler. Monthly digests of new exercise science research. |
| [Barbell Medicine](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/) | Practitioner blog | High | Evidence-based programming and pain science. Good for "when to worry" questions. |

## What the Research Actually Says (verified claims)

| Claim | Evidence | Source |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Progressive overload is required for continued adaptation | Strong (multiple meta-analyses) | ACSM position stand |
| Beginners need only 6-10 sets/muscle/week | Strong (Schoenfeld 2017 dose-response) | Meta-analysis |
| 2x/week frequency > 1x for same volume | Moderate (Schoenfeld 2016) | Meta-analysis |
| Hypertrophy occurs across ≥30% 1RM if taken near failure | Strong (trivial ES 0.03 between loads) | Schoenfeld 2017 |
| Linear progression is optimal for beginners | Consensus (novice neural adaptation) | NSCA, practical consensus |
| Training to failure is NOT required | Strong (Vieira 2022, no sig. difference) | Meta-analysis |
| 50% of beginners quit by 6 months | Observational | Multiple adherence studies |
| Neural adaptations dominate first 4-8 weeks | Established (strength gains without hypertrophy) | Motor learning literature |

## What's Myth (commonly repeated, not evidence-supported)

| Myth | Reality | Source |
|------|---------|--------|
| Anabolic window (must eat within 30 min) | Total daily protein matters more than timing | Schoenfeld 2013 meta-analysis |
| Women get bulky from heavy weights | Testosterone levels make this physiologically unlikely | Endocrine literature |
| Spot reduction (ab exercises burn belly fat) | Fat loss is systemic, not localized | Multiple RCTs |
| Machines are inferior to free weights | Both produce similar hypertrophy; machines are safer for beginners | Meta-analyses |
| You must train to failure | 1-3 RIR (reps in reserve) produces equivalent results | Vieira 2022 |
| More complex programs are better | Beginners respond to anything; complexity adds adherence cost | Practical consensus |

## What This System CANNOT Teach

| Domain | Why | What to do instead |
|--------|-----|-------------------|
| Exercise form/technique | Requires proprioception + visual feedback | In-person coach, form check videos, r/formcheck |
| Effort calibration (RPE feel) | Must be experienced physically | Practice with simple movements, calibrate over weeks |
| Complex movement patterns (squat, deadlift) | Supervised > self-directed for complex lifts (Gavanda 2025) | Consider 2-3 coaching sessions for compound lifts |
| Motivation/adherence | Not a knowledge problem — it's a systems/habit problem | Implementation intentions, accountability partner, environment design |
